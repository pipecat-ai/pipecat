Three texts,
one cursor.

How Pipecat keeps what the LLM wrote, what the screen renders, and what the TTS speaks in sync — word by word, while the audio is still playing.

① LLM TEXT — the conversation context ② SEGMENT TEXT — downstream consumers ③ TTS TEXT — the provider

Learn these three colours now. They label every table, diagram and cursor for the rest of the deck.

1.1 One response, three consumers

The same sentence has to be three different strings.

A bot prompted to wrap cards in <card> and code in <code> produces exactly one thing:

Your card is <card>1234-5678-9012-3456</card>. Run <code>npm install</code> to start.
ConsumerWantsWhy
① Conversation context Your card is <card>1234-…-3456</card>. Run <code>npm install</code> to start. The LLM must see its own tags next turn, or it stops producing them
② The user's screen Your card is XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-3456. + a highlighted code block The UI renders and redacts; raw tags are noise
③ The TTS provider Your card is <spell>1234-…-3456</spell>. — and nothing for the code Digits must be spelled out; code must not be read aloud

1.2 What you get back

The provider reports only the words it actually spoke.

No offsets. No segment ids. No tags. Just tokens, in order, with timestamps — and they are the only signal available. Everything in this deck exists to answer three questions for each one.

Question one

Where are we?

Which position in the displayed text, so a UI can move a highlight.

Question two

What goes in the context?

Which span of the LLM's own text, so the tags survive the round trip.

Question three

Can this frame go now?

In what order, relative to everything else in the turn.

Three questions, three layers — TextSegmentMap, WordCompletionTracker, AggregatedFrameSequencer. But first, what happened when they didn't exist.

II

What was broken

Four failures, four provider quirks — and one capability that only works once they are solved.

2.1 Context drift 2.2 Out-of-order frames 2.3 No word ↔ sentence link 2.4 Useless RTVI transforms 2.5 Provider reality 2.6 Text transformations — the feature

2.1 The context drifted from what the LLM wrote

The bot slowly forgets its own conventions.

The text appended to the context was the text that came back from the TTS — so everything that made the output speakable was lost on the round trip.

Before — context receives
Your card is 1234 5678 9012 3456
Next turn: the LLM sees no tags and stops emitting them. The feature quietly decays over a handful of turns.
Now — context receives
Your card is <card>1234-5678-9012-3456</card>
The LLM sees its own convention and keeps it.

Why this one is the worst: it fails silently and slowly. Nothing errors, nothing logs, and the regression shows up as "the model got lazy" several turns later.

2.2 Skipped frames arrived out of order

A frame with no audio has nothing to wait for.

Configure skip_aggregator_types=["code"] and that frame is never synthesized — no audio, no word events. So it was pushed the moment it appeared, landing before the sentence that precedes it.

LLM:      "Run this:"  →  <code>npm install</code>  →  "Then reload."

Context:  <code>npm install</code>      ← arrived first, nothing to wait for
          "Run this:"                   ← spoken later
          "Then reload."

The transcript reads out of order — and on interruption it is worse: text that was never spoken could still be recorded as if it had been.

2.3 Highlighting spoken words was not possible

Two streams, no correspondence between them.

What the client renders

AggregationType.SENTENCE

Whole sentence frames, arriving before synthesis.

What arrives as speech progresses

AggregationType.WORD

Word frames — carrying no indication of which sentence they belong to, or where inside it.

The client could not turn a stream of words into a highlight moving through a rendered sentence. There was simply no key linking the two.

2.4 RTVI bot_output_transforms were useless word-by-word

You cannot redact a card number that arrives in four pieces.

Client-side transforms operate on a segment. Receiving text word by word — with no segment identity, and no notion of "spoken so far" versus "remaining" — left nothing coherent to transform.

Event1234
Arrives as1234567890123456
Transform sees"1234""5678""9012""3456"
Can it redact?nononono

Four disconnected events, no segment, no split. To produce XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-3456 a transform needs the whole segment plus the current spoken split — which is exactly what did not exist.

2.5 Problems found along the way

Then reality: providers, streaming, concurrency.

ProblemWhat happens
Streamed tokens In TOKEN mode the service dispatches word-sized chunks, but tracking and progress need whole sentences — which are not known until a boundary is confirmed
Concurrent contexts Two back-to-back TTSSpeakFrames on a websocket service can be in flight at once; their word streams interleave and must not consume each other's slots
Straddling tokens One provider token completes one frame and starts the next (1111And), so a single event must produce output for two slots, in the right order
Dropped events The provider silently never reports a word it spoke, leaving a frame — and everything queued behind it — waiting forever

These four are why tests/test_word_completion_tracker.py is 203 tests, most of them a regression corpus of real provider behaviour.

2.6 And a feature: text transformations

Rewritten for the provider. For nobody else.

TTS engines read what you give them, and written text is often not what you want spoken. $42.50 can come out "dollar forty two point five zero"; 1994 as "one thousand nine hundred ninety four" when the sentence means a year. So Pipecat ships a bundle of transforms that rewrite the text on its way to the provider — and nowhere else.

TransformInputSent to TTS

expand_percentagesUp 12.5%twelve point five percent

email_to_speecha@b.coma at b dot com

expand_numbersroom 1994room 1 9 9 4
normalize_acronymsI work at IBMI work at I B M

Which ones you need depends on the provider

This is not a fix for a bug — it's a way to make sure all TTS providers are going to work the same way.

So it's opt-in, transform by transform

expand_numbers ships off — digits are often better read as digits. You enable what your provider gets wrong, per segment type if needed: tts.add_text_transformer(fn, "credit_card").

3.3 The guarantee that makes it useful

One rule holds after every single word.

progress.accumulated_text + progress.remaining_text  ==  AggregatedTextFrame.text

Exactly — character for character. Never a paraphrase, never a normalised copy, never off by a space. The two halves are always a clean split of the string the segment frame is already carrying.

  • Highlight the first half, leave the second plain → word highlighting
  • Redact both halves → redaction that keeps advancing
  • Concatenate them → the original back, exactly

That is what makes progress frames usable by anything, without coordination. A consumer holding the segment frame never has to guess how the text was transformed on its way to the TTS.

Only raw_pos is a real cursor. Through an unchanged segment the other two move by a count — however many letters and digits the word spent. Through a rewritten one they are held, then jump to the end of the original span in one step, because no position halfway through $42.50 means anything. Nothing ever speaks a </card>, so the walk ends on a beat that is not a word at all: once the frame is done, the tracker claims whatever llm text has left — which is how a closing tag reaches the conversation context. 4.1 Building the map

Diff the two texts once, at word level.

difflib.SequenceMatcher compares TTS text against segment text, tokenized on whitespace keeping the whitespace as tokens so offsets stay exact. Each opcode becomes one TextSegment.

OpcodeMeaning
equalIdentical on both sides — the common case
replaceRewritten — every value transform and added tag
deleteIn the original, not in the TTS text
insertIn the TTS text, not in the original

All four occur and the code treats them the same way — none is a special case.

original = 'Your balance is $42.50'
tts      = 'Your balance is forty-two
            dollars and fifty cents'

replace  '$42.50'
         → 'forty-two dollars…'

  tts='forty-two dollars…',
  original_start=16,
  original_end=22)

4.2 is_transformed · split_markup_runs

All-or-nothing, and how that cost is contained.

A segment is transformed when…

  • the letters and digits differ — $42.50 vs forty-two…
  • the number of words differs
  • the TTS side contains a tag — even if the spoken words are identical

Rule three is why <phoneme…>Siobhan</phoneme> counts: the raw cursor must cross tag characters the other texts do not have.

So cut the tag out into its own segment

split_markup_runs("I love to count <spell>1234</spell>.")
# → ["I love to count ",
#    "<spell>1234</spell>."]
"I love to count "plain — cursors move word by word
"<spell>1234</spell>."all-or-nothing — lands as one

Only equal opcodes can be split this way — both sides hold the same text, so a single offset cuts both. Where the sides differ there is no shared offset.

4.3 advance_by_alnums

Counting is what attaches tags to words.

Nothing decides which words own which tags. One loop counts letters and digits and steps over everything else — where the tags land simply follows from that.

Rule one

A <...> tag is crossed for free, without using up any of the count — so an opening tag travels with the word that follows it.

llm: Your card is <card>4111</card>
                  ↑ costs 0    ↑ 4 digits = 4
llm_pos = 23, not 17

Rule two

Punctuation right after the count runs out is taken too — so a trailing comma travels with the word before it.

provider reports:  "Yeah"
context records:   "Yeah,"

The closing tag is the one thing the loop deliberately stops short of — it is swept up by WordCompletionTracker when the frame completes. Why no diff for llm_text? It holds the same letters and digits as the segment text, in the same order, differing only in what wraps them. So the map never finds positions in it — it only counts.

4.4 _classify_hop

Challenge: Provider tokens are messy.

Three strategies, first hit wins.

Provider behaviourRemainingTokenStrategy
Plain wordhello worldhello1 literal
Carries its own space (Inworld)" world"" world"1 literal
Skipped punctuation it didn't speak", I can help"I1 skip offset
Added a terminal periodaccount and moreaccount.1 trim
Lowercased the wordSQL is greatsql2 folded
Stripped a diacriticcafé opencafe2 folded
Normalized an apostrophedon’t worrydon't2 folded
Reported without its tags<spell>1234</spell>12343 markup

Foreign token (dropped event)hello worldgoodbyenone

1 · literal

Character for character, at three skip offsets: as-is, past whitespace, past all punctuation.

2 · variation folded

Case, accents, typography. Folding is 1:1 on length, so an offset found in the folded copy is valid in the original. Requires a word boundary — otherwise account matches Accountant.

3 · markup stripped

Tags removed from both sides, then the offset translated back.

The whole thing is stateless — worked out fresh on every call, with no tag parsing and nothing remembered between words.

4.5 The four outcomes

Two end the walk. Two carry the token onward.

OutcomeMeaningEffect
PLACEDToken fits inside this segmentMove to the end of the match, stop
CROSSESThis segment holds only the start of the tokenFinish the segment, carry the rest of the token onward
EXHAUSTEDNothing here can be spokenFinish the segment, carry the whole token onward
NO_MATCHToken does not belong hereStep past leading punctuation, stop
tts='Hello five'  original='Hello 5'

token 'Hello five'
  ├─ seg0: CROSSES, 6 chars → completes
  └─ seg1: PLACED → user_facing_pos 7

# one token completed two segments and
# crossed a transform, in one call

The symbol case

A token with no letters or digits — an emoji, or a symbol the provider swapped (ElevenLabs reports → as -) — cannot be matched by any strategy.

It passes the dry run via _symbol_belongs_here so the slot is not force-completed over it, then classifies NO_MATCH: the raw cursor nudges past ' → ' so the next token isn't blocked, while the segment cursor holds. Accepted without being placed.

5.4 AggregatedFrameSequencer

Every frame takes a slot — spoken or not.

slot 1 · spoken
"Here is the code:"
tracker ●   complete: ✗
slot 2 · skipped
npm install
no tracker
slot 3 · spoken
"Then reload."
tracker ●   complete: ✗
Slot at headAction
Spoken and completePop it, keep walking
SkippedEmit it, pop it, keep walking
Spoken and not completeStop

That single rule is the whole ordering guarantee. On interruption clear() drops the queue, so a skipped frame whose preceding sentence was never finished is never recorded either.

callframes returned
process_word("Here")TTSText, Progress
process_word("is")TTSText, Progress
process_word("the")TTSText, Progress
process_word("code")TTSText, Progress, AggregatedTextFrame("npm install")

The code block is released by the very word that completes the sentence in front of it — same call, correct position.

5.5 Concurrent contexts

Two utterances in flight, one timeline.

Two back-to-back TTSSpeakFrames on a websocket service overlap — run_tts returns before synthesis finishes. Route words to "the first incomplete slot" and B's words get eaten by A's slot.

FieldScopeLifetime
_slotsGlobalThe one ordered timeline across all contexts — ordering is the point, so this stays a single list
_context_append_to_contextPer contextCreated at registration, removed by force_complete. Its presence marks the context live
_streaming_contextsPer contextOnly while a sentence accumulates from tokens; released by finalize
seq.process_word("beta",  pts=1000, context_id="ctxB")   # → TTSTextFrame('beta')
seq.process_word("alpha", pts=1000, context_id="ctxA")   # → TTSTextFrame('alpha')

ctxB's word reaches ctxB's slot even though ctxA's slot sits earlier and is still incomplete. A word for a context with no live entry is dropped as stale — that is what stops timestamps delivered seconds after an interruption from bleeding into the next turn. A None context id matches any slot (legacy providers, where concurrency cannot occur).

5.6 Token mode (streaming)

The sentence is always known one token late.

In TextAggregationMode.TOKEN the service dispatches word-sized chunks — but tracking and progress need a sentence, and a boundary is only confirmed by lookahead: the first non-whitespace character of the next sentence.

callframes returned
register_spoken("Hi")— nothing
register_spoken(" there!")— boundary not yet confirmed
register_spoken(" Bye")AggregatedTextFrame('Hi there!')
process_word("Hi")TTSText, Progress
finalize("ctx1")AggregatedTextFrame(' Bye')

finalize is what rescues a response that ends without terminal punctuation.

Buffered words

Because promotion lags, a word can arrive before its slot exists. It is parked in _buffered_words, then replayed on the next promotion. _drain_buffered_words snapshots and clears before replaying, so a word that still matches nothing is re-buffered rather than looping.

All three are driven from TTSService.
