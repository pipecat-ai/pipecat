- Added expressive conversation (alpha), turned on with `PipelineParams(enable_expressive_conversation=True)`: TTS services perform expression tags the LLM writes into its reply, instead of speaking them. The tag syntax and these APIs are likely to change between releases.
  - `<<mode>>` sets the delivery of the words that follow, such as `<<excited>>`, until the next mode tag or the end of the response, and `<<neutral>>` returns to the voice's baseline. `[[event]]` is a sound at one point, such as `[[laughs]]`, `[[pause 500]]` is a pause in milliseconds, and `^word^` stresses a word.
  - Each TTS service performs what its `ExpressionAdapter` supports and leaves out the rest, with a warning. Adapters ship for `CartesiaTTSService` and `CartesiaHttpTTSService`, `ElevenLabsDialogueTTSService`, `ElevenLabsHttpTTSService` on Eleven v3 and v4 models, `FishAudioTTSService` on S2 models, and `MiniMaxHttpTTSService` on speech-2.8 models. A service broadcasts what it can perform at start in a `TTSMetadataFrame`.
  - The context records the reply as the LLM wrote it, tags included. Clients receive each word's `modes` on `bot-output`. Events and pauses are part of what the bot speaks: they reach clients like spoken text, as `bot-output` segments of the `expression_event` and `expression_pause` types, announced in the order written and completed when performed, timed by the provider where it reports them.
  - Examples for each provider are in `examples/expressive-conversation/`.
